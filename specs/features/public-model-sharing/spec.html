Public model sharing

Status: Spec — under review · updated 2026-09-21

1. Problem and audience

Today a modeling built at e-footprint.boavizta.org lives at most 12 hours in an encrypted session cache, then disappears. The only way to persist or transmit one is a JSON file download. There is consequently no way to reference a modeling publicly: no stable URL to cite in an article, publish on a corporate transparency page, or hand to a peer reviewer.

This feature lets a user publish a modeling as a long-lived, public, forkable web page with a stable link, and lets anyone discover published modelings through a public, searchable index.

PersonaProblem solved
Company / product team practicing ecodesign Publish a transparent, living account of the footprint of their digital service and the effect of their ecodesign actions, updatable as the service evolves.
Green-IT writers and advocates (including the maintainer) Illustrate articles with actual, explorable modelings rather than screenshots.
Journalists and researchers Cite a modeling that readers and peers can open, audit down to every hypothesis, and fork — with immutable version permalinks so a citation cannot silently change.
Readers / peers (no account) Consult a shared modeling read-only and fork it into their own session to challenge hypotheses, with zero sign-up friction.

Why now: e-footprint's differentiator is explainability — every value carries its derivation. That value is currently locked inside private sessions. Public sharing turns each published modeling into a demonstration of the methodology, compounding the tool's notoriety and the transparency norms it promotes in the green-IT ecosystem.

2. Success criteria

Each criterion is user-observable and testable.

  • Publish: a signed-in user can publish the modeling in their current session with a title, free-text tags, an optional link to the article or page that references it, and a free-text public author/organization name specific to that modeling. Publishing yields a stable public URL.
  • Personalize the sharing URL: the publish form suggests a URL suffix from the title and lets the publisher edit it before first publication, with a preview of the full URL, for example e-footprint.boavizta.org/shared/acme-streaming-footprint. Suffixes use lowercase letters, numbers and single hyphens between words, and must be unique across published modelings. Invalid or unavailable suffixes prevent publication with a clear message and preserve the form contents; availability is checked again when publishing. Once published, the URL is fixed, even if the title changes.
  • View without an account: anyone opening a share link sees the modeling read-only — objects, hypotheses, results and visualizations, with the usual explainability (every value's derivation reachable) — without signing in.
  • Personal viewing preferences: visitors can adjust interface-only settings, including Sankey display options and card order, directly at the shared URL without forking or signing in. The published interface settings provide the initial defaults; subsequent changes are saved in the visitor's session, scoped to that shared modeling, and survive navigation and reloads within that session. They do not change the published modeling or its interface defaults, affect another visitor's view, or carry over to other shared modelings. This applies to the owner viewing the shared page too. Read-only prevents changes to modeling inputs and structure, not personal presentation preferences.
  • Fork without an account: from a shared modeling, any visitor can load a copy into their own editing session and modify it freely. The fork is theirs; the original is untouched. Forking requires no account (an account is only needed to publish). The fork records the source modeling and exact source version so that, if later published, its public page links visibly back to that provenance. A published fork gets its own independently chosen sharing URL through the same publishing flow.
  • Update with history: the owner can publish a new version of their modeling. The chosen canonical link always shows the latest version; every previously published version remains reachable at an immutable permalink, and the page makes it visible which version is displayed and that history exists. Version permalinks extend the chosen URL, for example /shared/acme-streaming-footprint/versions/2.
  • Discover: a public index page lists all published modelings, searchable by title and filterable by tag. Each entry shows title, author name, tags, last-update date, and the article link when present.
  • Thin accounts: a user can create an account with an email address and password, and must prove control of that email before publishing. Email-and-password sign-in allows credentials to be shared by members managing a modeling as a team. The account exists solely to own published modelings; public author names belong to individual modelings and the account email is never shown publicly.
  • Erasure: a user can delete any of their published modelings, and can delete their account entirely (which deletes all their published modelings, breaking their share links); both actions take effect immediately and are user-triggered, with no Boavizta-volunteer involvement. The author owns the reference: publishing publicly does not strip them of control over their data. Forks already published by others are independent modelings and are unaffected.
  • Abuse resistance: publishing is quota-bound (per-account limits on number of published modelings, modeling size, and publish rate) so that a hostile script with a burner email cannot meaningfully grow the database. Exceeding a quota produces a clear, non-destructive error.
  • Moderation safety valve: each public modeling has a discreet email-based report link that includes its URL, and an account with moderation privileges can unpublish it. Administrators can grant or revoke that permission and promote other accounts to administrator; the maintainer starts as the initial administrator. V1 does not require an internal reporting or case-management workflow.
  • No private storage channel: there is no way to store a modeling server-side beyond the existing ephemeral session other than publishing it publicly (public by construction).

3. Scope

Capability-level only. File paths, class/method/parameter names, refactor instructions, internal hypotheses, and "mirror pattern X" code-level decisions belong in plan.html, not here.
In scope
  • Thin user accounts: email-and-password sign-up with email verification; sign-in and password recovery; account deletion.
  • Publishing the session's modeling as a public modeling: title, free-text tags, optional article backlink, and a free-text author name stored per modeling.
  • A title-derived, editable sharing-URL suffix before first publication, with full URL preview and format/availability validation; the chosen URL stays fixed thereafter.
  • Public by construction: every long-lived stored modeling is public and listed in the index. No private or unlisted drafts; the ephemeral session and the JSON download remain the only private workspaces.
  • Read-only public view of a shared modeling, with full explainability and results visualizations, accessible without an account.
  • Session-persisted personal viewing preferences on the shared page (including Sankey settings and card order), initialized from published defaults and isolated per visitor and shared modeling.
  • Fork-to-session from any shared modeling (any version), without an account, capturing the source modeling and version for visible provenance if the fork is later published.
  • Versioning: canonical latest URL + immutable per-version permalinks + visible version history.
  • Owner management: publish an update, edit metadata (title, tags, article link, author name), delete a published modeling.
  • Public index: search by title, filter by free-text tags, sorted listing with basic metadata. Tags remain unconstrained initially and may be consolidated once actual usage data shows which vocabulary is useful.
  • Anti-abuse: per-account quotas (count, size, rate) and verified-email gate on publishing.
  • Lightweight moderation: a visitor-facing email report link carrying the modeling URL; moderator unpublish capability; administrators can promote other accounts to administrator and grant or revoke moderation privileges, with the maintainer seeded as the initial administrator.
  • Legal surfaces: privacy notice, terms of use covering published-content ownership and the reuse implied by forking, consent at account creation.
  • Governance-doc updates shipped with the feature: amend specs/mission.md (public model library replaces the "no persistent per-user libraries" exclusion) and specs/roadmap.md.
Out of scope (this iteration)
  • Private or unlisted long-lived modelings, in any form.
  • Renaming a sharing URL after publication and redirects for renamed URLs; custom domains and account-based URL namespaces.
  • Multi-user collaborative editing of the same modeling (constitution §4; forking is the collaboration model).
  • Social features: comments, likes, follower feeds, user profile pages beyond the author name.
  • Full-text or semantic search inside modeling contents; search covers title and tags only.
  • Curating or consolidating the free-text tag vocabulary before usage data exists.
  • Third-party sign-in (OAuth/SSO).
  • Embeddable widgets for articles (readers click through to the public view).
  • DOI minting or formal academic-citation tooling (immutable permalinks are the v1 citation mechanism).
  • Subscription or activity-notification emails (e.g. "a modeling you forked was updated"). Account verification and password recovery emails remain in scope.
  • A stored moderation queue, dedicated dashboard, report tracking or statuses, automated acknowledgements, and a public response-time commitment.
  • Automatic content-quality review of published modelings (publication is self-service; moderation is reactive).

4. UX / interface

The share-a-model journey is the single mockup reference. It places publishing, anonymous reading, forking, owner updates, and discovery inside the surrounding application. It is a self-contained HTML file suitable for board review; its example values are illustrative and its interactive controls do not publish anything.

At a shared URL, the modeling stays read-only for everyone, including its owner. A visitor chooses Fork into my session to create an independent editable copy. An owner can instead choose Edit and publish an update to open a working copy associated with the existing publication. Both lead to the normal builder; neither changes the public version until an explicit publish action. Personal viewing preferences remain editable directly on the shared page.

New recommendations in the review questions below are labeled as proposals in the journey. They must not be mistaken for approved scope.

5. Constraints

  • Data minimization: required account information is the email address, password hash, and verification/authorization state. Public author names are modeling metadata. This is a description of the feature's required fields, not a claim that Boavizta has never processed personal data or that user-authored content cannot contain it. The privacy notice must also account for sessions, the email provider, operational logs, and backup retention; the board must validate the handling and erasure arrangements before production activation.
  • Board approval gates production. The feature must be deployable and demonstrable on pre-production (dev.e-footprint.boavizta.org) while the Boavizta board decision is pending; production activation is a deliberate, separate step.
  • Public content needs terms. Publishing implies anyone may view and fork. The terms of use must state the license/reuse regime of published modelings explicitly at publish time. A draft license regime lives in content-license.md in this feature folder; it requires Boavizta board validation alongside the personal-data pitch.
  • Longevity across schema evolution. Published modelings (including old version snapshots) are long-lived while the efootprint schema evolves; they must remain viewable and forkable after library upgrades, leveraging the existing upgrade path that session/JSON round-trips already rely on.
  • Bounded storage. Long-lived storage on Boavizta's hosting (Clever Cloud) is a new standing cost; quotas must keep it bounded and predictable. Quota values are a plan decision, referenced symbolically here.
  • Public views use the standard request lifecycle. Anonymous read-only views rebuild a ModelWeb from the serialized modeling data like editing requests do. They do not require a separate no-rebuild path; their defining difference is that they cannot mutate the published modeling. Saving a visitor's personal interface preferences is allowed, but must never write to published snapshots or shared interface defaults, including when the visitor is the owner.
  • Constitution: Clean Architecture layering and HTMX-first apply to all new surfaces; multi-user collaborative editing stays rejected (§4). No constitution amendment is required, but mission.md and roadmap.md must be amended deliberately alongside this feature (mission currently excludes persistent per-user model libraries).
  • Accessibility of trust signals: the public view must make it unmistakable which version is shown, when it was published, and by whom (author name), so citations are auditable.

6. Decisions and remaining review questions

  • Authentication: email and password, with email verification and password recovery. This is easier for members of a team to share when they jointly manage published modelings.
  • Author name: free text stored per modeling, not a single account-level display name.
  • Tags: free text at launch. Consolidation or curation is deferred until actual usage provides evidence for a useful vocabulary.
  • Sharing URL: suggest a suffix from the title, allow customization with availability checks before first publication, then keep it fixed across title edits and version updates. Published forks choose their own URL. Renaming/redirects, custom domains and account-based namespaces are outside V1.
  • Read-only scope: modeling inputs and structure stay read-only at shared URLs. Interface-only preferences remain editable and persist per visitor session and shared modeling, starting from published defaults; no fork or account is needed.
  • Moderation: V1 provides only a lightweight safety valve: a report-by-email link containing the modeling URL and an unpublish permission that administrators may grant or revoke. Administrators may promote other administrators, and the maintainer is the initial one. Moderation queues, dashboards, case tracking, statuses, acknowledgements, and response commitments are deferred until usage demonstrates a need.
  • Fork provenance: capture the source modeling and exact version at fork time and display that link if the fork is later published.
  • Content license: drafted in content-license.md (this folder) — CC BY 4.0 proposed. Remaining open point: Boavizta board validation, alongside the personal-data pitch.

Product decisions still to confirm

Review of 2026-09-21. The following are recommendations for discussion, not approved requirements. They close behavior gaps without adding a social or collaborative-editing layer.

QuestionSuggested V1 behavior
What if a fork or owner edit meets a full workspace? Use an available modeling slot; otherwise ask the user which modeling to replace, with an export option and an explicit confirmation. Cancel leaves everything intact. Simply opening a shared page never replaces the editing workspace.
What exactly is immutable after a library upgrade? Preserve the published inputs, attribution, and published headline results with the calculation-engine version. Distinguish any newly recomputed exploration from the original results. Decide whether complete historical-result replay is required; schema upgrades alone do not guarantee unchanged numerical results.
Can changing a title or author rewrite an old citation? Allow current listing metadata to change, but snapshot title, author, license and initial presentation with each version. A version permalink and its citation retain that context. Decide how later attribution-removal requests are handled.
What survives deletion or moderator unpublishing? Show a neutral unavailable page for direct/version links and remove the modeling from discovery. Reserve a retired URL so an old citation cannot point to somebody else's work. Forks retain source/version/license citation information, with an unavailable-source label; determine any retained personal attribution with the board. Decide whether moderation hides the whole publication and all versions, and how it can be restored.
How is growing version history bounded? Count retained versions and total stored bytes in the account's quota. Reject new publications/updates when full without pruning existing cited versions. Quota values, publication rate limits, and backup capacity belong in the plan.
What if two team members publish from the same old version? Check the base version before publishing an update. If the public version has advanced, keep the working copy and explain the conflict; require a deliberate resolution, with no automatic merge or silent overwrite. This also covers editing an older version.
How do personal preferences behave across versions and forks? Keep valid viewing preferences across versions of the same modeling; ignore preferences for objects no longer present. Offer “Reset to published view.” A fork starts with the visitor's current presentation. Confirm whether provenance must survive JSON export/import; recommending yes makes offline reuse traceable too.
Can an incomplete modeling be published? Recommend requiring computable results for V1. State the failing prerequisite and preserve the workspace and publication form when computation or publishing fails. Validate this gate explicitly; it previously appeared only in the mockups.

Operational decisions before production

Confirm the transactional-email provider and administrator, the moderation report inbox and responsible person, initial-admin setup, the license and embedded-data compatibility, privacy and retention terms, and the production approval. Brevo is a candidate based on public DNS, not a confirmed available service. Provider quotas, email retry behavior, expired verification links, password recovery, tag/URL length limits, search pagination, and empty/error states should be detailed in the plan.

Small UX suggestions: separate “Copy latest link” from “Copy this version link,” show a visible “Your view · saved for this session” indicator, and offer a reset to the published presentation. These are marked as proposals in the journey. A direct JSON download from the shared page is another useful option to confirm.
