Public model sharing

Status: Spec — under review · 2026-07-03

1. Problem and audience

Today a modeling built at e-footprint.boavizta.org lives at most 12 hours in an encrypted session cache, then disappears. The only way to persist or transmit one is a JSON file download. There is consequently no way to reference a modeling publicly: no stable URL to cite in an article, publish on a corporate transparency page, or hand to a peer reviewer.

This feature lets a user publish a modeling as a long-lived, public, forkable web page with a stable link, and lets anyone discover published modelings through a public, searchable index.

PersonaProblem solved
Company / product team practicing ecodesign Publish a transparent, living account of the footprint of their digital service and the effect of their ecodesign actions, updatable as the service evolves.
Green-IT writers and advocates (including the maintainer) Illustrate articles with actual, explorable modelings rather than screenshots.
Journalists and researchers Cite a modeling that readers and peers can open, audit down to every hypothesis, and fork — with immutable version permalinks so a citation cannot silently change.
Readers / peers (no account) Consult a shared modeling read-only and fork it into their own session to challenge hypotheses, with zero sign-up friction.

Why now: e-footprint's differentiator is explainability — every value carries its derivation. That value is currently locked inside private sessions. Public sharing turns each published modeling into a demonstration of the methodology, compounding the tool's notoriety and the transparency norms it promotes in the green-IT ecosystem.

2. Success criteria

Each criterion is user-observable and testable.

  • Publish: a signed-in user can publish the modeling in their current session with a title, a set of tags chosen from a curated list, an optional link to the article or page that references it, and a public author/organization name. Publishing yields a stable public URL.
  • View without an account: anyone opening a share link sees the modeling read-only — objects, hypotheses, results and visualizations, with the usual explainability (every value's derivation reachable) — without signing in.
  • Fork without an account: from a shared modeling, any visitor can load a copy into their own editing session and modify it freely. The fork is theirs; the original is untouched. Forking requires no account (an account is only needed to publish).
  • Update with history: the owner can publish a new version of their modeling. The canonical link always shows the latest version; every previously published version remains reachable at an immutable permalink, and the page makes it visible which version is displayed and that history exists.
  • Discover: a public index page lists all published modelings, searchable by title and filterable by tag. Each entry shows title, author name, tags, last-update date, and the article link when present.
  • Thin accounts: a user can create an account with an email address only, and must prove control of that email before publishing. The account exists solely to own published modelings; it stores no personal data beyond the email (the public author name is chosen by the user and displayed instead of the email, which is never shown publicly).
  • Erasure: a user can delete any of their published modelings, and can delete their account entirely (which deletes all their published modelings, breaking their share links); both actions take effect immediately and are user-triggered, with no Boavizta-volunteer involvement. The author owns the reference: publishing publicly does not strip them of control over their data. Forks already published by others are independent modelings and are unaffected.
  • Abuse resistance: publishing is quota-bound (per-account limits on number of published modelings, modeling size, and publish rate) so that a hostile script with a burner email cannot meaningfully grow the database. Exceeding a quota produces a clear, non-destructive error.
  • Moderation: any visitor can report a published modeling, and an administrator can unpublish one — covering illegal or abusive content without making volunteers a routine editorial bottleneck.
  • No private storage channel: there is no way to store a modeling server-side beyond the existing ephemeral session other than publishing it publicly (public by construction).

3. Scope

Capability-level only. File paths, class/method/parameter names, refactor instructions, internal hypotheses, and "mirror pattern X" code-level decisions belong in plan.html, not here.
In scope
  • Thin user accounts: email-only sign-up with email verification; sign-in; account deletion; a user-chosen public author/organization name.
  • Publishing the session's modeling as a public modeling: title, curated tags, optional article backlink, author name.
  • Public by construction: every long-lived stored modeling is public and listed in the index. No private or unlisted drafts; the ephemeral session and the JSON download remain the only private workspaces.
  • Read-only public view of a shared modeling, with full explainability and results visualizations, accessible without an account.
  • Fork-to-session from any shared modeling (any version), without an account.
  • Versioning: canonical latest URL + immutable per-version permalinks + visible version history.
  • Owner management: publish an update, edit metadata (title, tags, article link), delete a published modeling.
  • Public index: search by title, filter by curated tags, sorted listing with basic metadata.
  • Anti-abuse: per-account quotas (count, size, rate) and verified-email gate on publishing.
  • Moderation: visitor-facing report action; administrator unpublish capability.
  • Legal surfaces: privacy notice, terms of use covering published-content ownership and the reuse implied by forking, consent at account creation.
  • Governance-doc updates shipped with the feature: amend specs/mission.md (public model library replaces the "no persistent per-user libraries" exclusion) and specs/roadmap.md.
Out of scope (this iteration)
  • Private or unlisted long-lived modelings, in any form.
  • Multi-user collaborative editing of the same modeling (constitution §4; forking is the collaboration model).
  • Social features: comments, likes, follower feeds, user profile pages beyond the author name.
  • Full-text or semantic search inside modeling contents; search covers title and tags only.
  • User-created tags (the tag list is curated; extending it is a maintainer action).
  • Third-party sign-in (OAuth/SSO), password-recovery flows beyond what the chosen auth mechanism needs.
  • Embeddable widgets for articles (readers click through to the public view).
  • DOI minting or formal academic-citation tooling (immutable permalinks are the v1 citation mechanism).
  • Email notifications of any kind (e.g. "a modeling you forked was updated").
  • Automatic content-quality review of published modelings (publication is self-service; moderation is reactive).

5. Constraints

  • Data minimization is the pitch. Boavizta is a French non-profit that has never handled personal data. The only personal data stored is the account email (plus the user-chosen, deliberately public author name). This must remain literally true — it is the core of the case to be made to the Boavizta board. GDPR obligations follow: consent at sign-up, a privacy notice, self-service erasure, and a data-processing register entry for Boavizta.
  • Board approval gates production. The feature must be deployable and demonstrable on pre-production (dev.e-footprint.boavizta.org) while the Boavizta board decision is pending; production activation is a deliberate, separate step.
  • Public content needs terms. Publishing implies anyone may view and fork. The terms of use must state the license/reuse regime of published modelings explicitly at publish time. A draft license regime lives in content-license.md in this feature folder; it requires Boavizta board validation alongside the personal-data pitch.
  • Longevity across schema evolution. Published modelings (including old version snapshots) are long-lived while the efootprint schema evolves; they must remain viewable and forkable after library upgrades, leveraging the existing upgrade path that session/JSON round-trips already rely on.
  • Bounded storage. Long-lived storage on Boavizta's hosting (Clever Cloud) is a new standing cost; quotas must keep it bounded and predictable. Quota values are a plan decision, referenced symbolically here.
  • Read traffic must be cheap. A share link may sit in a widely-read article; anonymous read-only views must not inherit the per-request model-rebuild cost of the editing session, and must not degrade builder performance for active sessions.
  • Constitution: Clean Architecture layering and HTMX-first apply to all new surfaces; multi-user collaborative editing stays rejected (§4). No constitution amendment is required, but mission.md and roadmap.md must be amended deliberately alongside this feature (mission currently excludes persistent per-user model libraries).
  • Accessibility of trust signals: the public view must make it unmistakable which version is shown, when it was published, and by whom (author name), so citations are auditable.

6. Open questions

  • Auth mechanism: passwordless magic-link vs. email + password. Outbound email is a new operational dependency for Boavizta hosting either way (email verification gates publishing), so the choice is purely about sign-in UX and whether a credential hash is stored at all. Leaning passwordless (nothing to store, nothing to reset); settle in plan.html.
  • Author name placement: one display name per account vs. free-text per modeling (a consultant publishing for several clients would want per-modeling).
  • Initial curated tag list: which tags ship at launch, and what the process is for extending the list.
  • Moderation policy: who among Boavizta volunteers holds the unpublish capability, and what response commitment (if any) is stated publicly.
  • Content license: drafted in content-license.md (this folder) — CC BY 4.0 proposed. Remaining open point: Boavizta board validation, alongside the personal-data pitch.
  • Fork provenance: when a fork is later published, should it visibly link back to the modeling (and version) it was forked from? Cheap to capture at fork time, impossible to reconstruct later.
